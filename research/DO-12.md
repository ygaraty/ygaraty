## Question

A synchronous sharded update waits for every hop of its chain. It is now 4.64 s (experiment 61), and the chain's latency sets most of that. DiLoCo (Douillard et al. 2023) and Streaming DiLoCo let learners take many local steps between exchanges. Obeidi et al. (2026, arXiv:2601.02360) build learners as pipelines of weak participants, averaged rarely.

Ygaraty's 30.3M model fits in one sharded island but not in one weak worker. **Do two islands of 8 workers, each running its own synchronous sharded updates and averaged every H updates, reach a fixed held-out loss sooner in wall time than one island of 16?**

This leaves the paper's exactness invariant on purpose. The result is time to loss, not seconds per update.

## Prediction, recorded before any code

- **Per island:** an 8-worker island's update is 1.2–1.6× the 16-worker update. Experiment 36 measured 25.3 s against 17.6 s at 8 against 16 workers before later changes; with one layer per fast worker and handoffs, fewer workers costs less.
- **Throughput:** two islands process twice the pairs per island-update, so 1.25–1.65× the pairs per second of one 16-worker island.
- **Averaging:** at H = 25 the averaging costs 0.05–0.2 in held-out loss at equal pairs, early in training. At H = 100 it costs more.
- **Wall time:** H = 25 reaches the 16-worker island's 32,768-pair loss in 10–30% less wall time. The outer step's traffic is negligible at H ≥ 25: each owner uploads its piece's pseudo-gradient, about 2–8 MB, once per H updates.

## What has to be built

1. **Island jobs.** One coordinator process per island, on the same server on different ports, or a `--island K` mode of `distributed_translation`. Each island has its own job, relay prefix and owners, and the same data order offset by island: island k reads batches k, k+K, ….
2. **Outer step.**
   - After every H island updates, each island's owners publish their weights (the existing `owner/{tag}/publish` tasks).
   - An outer process computes the mean pseudo-gradient (initial − final) across islands per piece and applies DiLoCo's outer Nesterov step (learning rate 0.7, momentum 0.9, as in experiment 43).
   - It writes the new weights as each island's next `owner/{tag}/load`. Owners keep their Adam moments locally (DiLoCo's inner state).
3. **A synchronous barrier between islands at the outer step only.** A slow island delays the other; record that time.
4. **A reference.** `ygaraty.diloco` already runs DiLoCo replicas of the Transformer (`ygaraty/diloco/transformer.py`). Use it to check, on loopback with 2 replicas, that one outer step of the island implementation equals the replica implementation's to float64 tolerance.

## Design

**Stage 1:** run `RUNBOOK-E1a.md` as written: 12 jobs, about $35. It gives the sharded and replica rows at uplink ratios 100/100, 100/20 and 100/5, and the loss-5.0 crossing times.

**Stage 2:** experiment 61's pool (8 c-2 weak and 8 c-8 strong, nyc3), handoffs on, emulated 100/20 Mb/s, 50 ms links. Each island gets 4 weak and 4 strong workers.

| Arm | Islands | Workers per island | H |
|---|---|---|---|
| S | 1 | 16 | — (experiment 61's arm B, rerun for the learning curve) |
| I25 | 2 | 8 | 25 |
| I100 | 2 | 8 | 100 |

- Each arm trains 32,768 pairs in total, 512 to 1,100 updates depending on the arm.
- Held-out loss every 4,096 pairs, from published snapshots evaluated afterwards, as the runbook does.
- One repeat per arm, because each job is long. The learning curves give the spread over evaluation points.

## Gate

- **Primary measure:** wall time to S's held-out loss at 32,768 pairs (interpolated between evaluations).
- **Counts as a lever:** an island arm reaches it in at least 20% less wall time.
- **Report also:**
  - seconds per island-update;
  - outer-step time, including the barrier;
  - the pseudo-gradient bytes per owner;
  - the held-out loss at equal pairs.

## Trigger

**Stage 1:** follow `papers/uplink-bound/RUNBOOK-E1a.md` (pool, commands and "Verify first" items).

**Stage 2:**
```bash
git pull && pip install -e . && python -m unittest tests.test_islands      # outer step equals ygaraty.diloco on loopback
ygaraty experiment pods up do8 --provider digitalocean --location nyc3 --server g-8vcpu-32gb --workers 8 --worker-size c-2 --minutes 300 --max-hourly 2
ygaraty experiment pods up do8 --workers 16 --worker-size c-8 --max-hourly 2
ygaraty experiment pods ship do8
# server: bash /root/remote.sh bootstrap   workers: bash /root/remote.sh worker-setup
# S: as experiment 61 arm B with --steps sized to 32,768 pairs
# islands (on the server):
YGARATY_HANDOFF=1 YGARATY_ISLANDS=2 YGARATY_OUTER_EVERY=25 YGARATY_OUTER_LR=0.7 YGARATY_OUTER_MOMENTUM=0.9 ./scripts/remote.sh serve-islands
# each worker joins its island: wN for N in 0-3, 8-11 -> island 0; 4-7, 12-15 -> island 1
YGARATY_SHAPE=1 YGARATY_DOWN_MBPS=100 YGARATY_UP_MBPS=20 YGARATY_RTT_MS=50 ./scripts/remote.sh worker <coordinator:port-of-island> <relay> <job> <token> wN
ygaraty experiment pods fetch do8 && ygaraty experiment pods down do8
```

`serve-islands` and `YGARATY_ISLANDS` are part of what has to be built. Pass job tokens with experiment 43's script, not on the command line.

## Cost and limits

- **Cost:** stage 1 about $35 (the runbook's estimate). Stage 2 is about 3–4 hours of experiment 61's pool, about $8–12.
- **Limits:**
  - Two islands only, early training, one repeat.
  - The data split between islands and the outer optimizer settings come from experiment 43 (CIFAR), not tuned for translation.
  - Losing an island's owner stops that island, as today.
