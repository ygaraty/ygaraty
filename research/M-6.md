## Question

OPUS-MT's en→Romance model has exactly our layer shape: d = 512, FFN 2048, 8 heads. It has 6 layers per side against our 3, and a 65,001-entry vocabulary against our roughly 16k.

Starting from 3 of its layers per side would turn our training into continued training of a model that already translates. That is a different claim from the paper's training from scratch: the 4×10¹⁹-FLOP budget no longer applies, and the question becomes how much a volunteer pool can add to an open model.

Its vocabulary layer alone is about 33M parameters, more than our whole model. [M-3](M-3.md)'s factorized vocabulary is what makes it affordable: a rank-128 truncated SVD of the teacher's embedding is about 8.4M parameters.

**Started from 3 of OPUS-MT's layers per side, with its tokenizer and a rank-128 factorized embedding, how many pairs does the model need to reach the from-scratch model's chrF at 32,768 pairs?**

## Prediction

- **Pairs:** under 1/10 of 32,768, because the layers already translate.
- **Which layers:** keeping the first and last of each side, plus one in the middle (0, 3, 5), beats keeping the first three (0, 1, 2), following "Transformer Layers as Painters" (2024): the first and last layers matter most, middle layers are interchangeable.
- **Truncation:** rank 128 costs some chrF at step 0 against the full embedding, and recovers within a few thousand pairs.

- **Loss is not comparable across tokenizers.** Compare chrF, and held-out loss per target *character* (summed token NLL divided by characters), not per token.
- **Report also:** chrF at step 0, the FLORES-200 en–pt devtest result for both arms (outside OPUS, see Limits), and the parameter count.

## Protocol

1. **Architecture match first.** Our model is pre-norm, ReLU, with learned positions up to 64 tokens. Marian is, as far as I know, post-norm, with sinusoidal positions and a scaled embedding; check its `config.json` (activation, norm placement, `scale_embedding`, positions). Add a `--marian-compatible` architecture flag to `batch_learning` and the reference, and check that loading all 6+6 layers reproduces the teacher's logits on 10 sentences to float32 tolerance.
2. **Weights to load:**
   - encoder and decoder layers by index;
   - tokenizer: the teacher's SentencePiece models, as a new dataset version tokenized with them;
   - embedding: E ≈ U·Σ·Vᵀ truncated to r = 128, split as A = U·Σ^½ (V×128) and B = Σ^½·Vᵀ (128×512), tied as in M-3.
3. **Arms:**

   | Arm | Start |
   |---|---|
   | S | from scratch, our tokenizer (experiment 67's Adam arm; reuse it) |
   | W035 | OPUS-MT layers 0, 3, 5 per side, rank-128 embedding |
   | W012 | layers 0, 1, 2 per side, rank-128 embedding |
   | W035-full | layers 0, 3, 5, full embedding (memory permitting; an upper reference) |

4. **Runs:** 2 seeds, 32,768 pairs, chrF every 2,048 pairs on 512 deduplicated held-out pairs (greedy), final chrF on the whole split. Learning rate 1e-4 for the warm arms; check 5e-4 on one seed.
5. **Needs [M-3](M-3.md)'s `--factor-vocab` code**, not its result: run M-6 even if M-3 fails at our vocabulary, since a pretrained embedding may truncate better than one learned from scratch.

## Limits known in advance

- **Contamination:** OPUS-MT was trained on OPUS corpora, and OPUS-100's held-out pairs may be among them. That would flatter every warm arm. The FLORES-200 devtest evaluation is required before any claim.
- **A different claim:** this measures continued training, not training from scratch.
