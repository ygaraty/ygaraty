## Question

The paper's claim is that a sharded training update over home links is bound by its chain of dependent hops. It was measured at 30.3M parameters. Phi-4 now runs split over two owners with the reference's tokens (experiments 81 and 82).

LoRA fine-tuning is the cheapest real training of that model:
- **Owners keep their weights frozen** and train small low-rank adapters (rank 8–16 on the attention and MLP projections), with optimizer state only for those.
- **The chain is 2 stages each way:** forward A→B, loss, backward B→A. Each crossing carries a batch's activations or their gradients.

**Can the pipeline fine-tune Phi-4 with LoRA, with losses and adapter gradients matching a PyTorch reference, and is a step bound by its crossings or by compute, on GPUs in one data center and across the internet?**

## Prediction

- **Correctness:** loss within 1×10⁻³ and adapter gradients within 1×10⁻³ relative of the PyTorch reference (bf16 weights, float32 adapters and head) over 20 steps.
- **H100 pair in one data center** (experiment 81, 7 ms of network per token): a step of 4 × 512 tokens is compute-bound. Crossings are under 5% of the step.
- **Across the internet** (about 75 ms per crossing, experiment 82), with 4-bit weights on 8 GB GPUs: memory limits the batch to 1 × 256 tokens. The step is about 2.5–4 s, set by unpacking and backward compute rather than by the 4 crossings (about 0.3 s). So at 14B the chain matters less than at 30M: each hop carries far more compute.

- **Stage 1 (required):** real Phi-4, losses within 1×10⁻³ of the reference over 20 steps, 0 retries.
- **Diagnostic, not pass/fail:** each step's time split into compute per owner, crossings, and scheduling, for each setting. This is the evidence for or against the chain bound at 14B.

## Protocol

1. **Code:**
   - a backward pass through `phi4_kernels` for the owned layers (attention, MLP, RMSNorm, rotary), with frozen base weights;
   - LoRA adapters (A, B) on `qkv_proj`, `o_proj`, `gate_up_proj` and `down_proj`, held and stepped by their owner with Adam;
   - a cross-entropy loss on the head;
   - a PyTorch reference: transformers' Phi-4 with the same adapters, same init seed.
2. **Data:** 2,048 en→pt pairs from opus100-en-pt as chat-formatted examples. Loss on the answer tokens only.
3. **Stage 0:** tiny model, float64, 1 and 2 owners, 5 steps against the reference.
4. **Stage 1:** 2 GPUs of at least 40 GB in one data center (H100 or as available), bf16 base, float32 adapters, batch 4 × 512, 20 steps, timing over the last 15.
5. **Stage 2 (optional):** 2 × 8 GB GPUs on separate hosts with 4-bit base weights, batch as memory allows, 20 steps.

## Cost and limits

- **Cost:** stage 1 is about 1–2 hours of 2 GPUs, about $6–12 on H100 Droplets. Stage 2 is about $1. Confirm a budget and teardown deadline before provisioning.
- **Limits:**
  - 20 steps measure correctness and step time, not fine-tuning quality.
  - LoRA's chain is the same as full training's, but its optimizer state and gradient traffic are much smaller.
