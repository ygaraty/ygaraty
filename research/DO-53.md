## Question

For each stage, how is the per-token decode time divided among kernel families? How close are the weight matvecs to the GPU's measured bandwidth?

## Context

[Experiment 97](097-qwen35-webrtc-direct-links.md) is the reference inference pipeline. It decodes at 83 ms per token: stage A 36–38 ms, stage B 39–40 ms, link 2–3 ms. Compute dominates.

Rough estimate (unverified): ~2.4 GB of Q4_K_M weights per stage at ~360 GB/s (the 3060's spec) gives ~7 ms per stage at the memory roofline, so we may be 4–5× off. The Pooled project ([github.com/Nehanth/pooled](https://github.com/Nehanth/pooled), MIT) measured the same split on its engine: matvecs at the roofline, plus ~30 ms per token of small kernels, encoding, submit and readback. We don't know which of these dominates for us.

Related: [104](104-matvec-rows-per-thread.md), [105](105-matvec-policy-in-stages.md) and [108](108-batched-decode-rows-per-thread.md) tuned the matvec kernels, but none broke the step time down by kernel family.

## Gate

A diagnostic: the verdict is **Found**, with the breakdown. Proposed acceptance, to freeze before the first run:

- The bandwidth probe is repeatable: 5 runs within 5% of their median.
- The breakdown closes: the step time minus the sum of attributed drops (the unattributed remainder) is reported, not hidden. If it exceeds 20% of the step time, the breakdown is labeled incomplete.
- Each attributed drop is the median of at least 5 runs and is reported with its spread; drops within that spread are reported as unresolved, not zero.
- Timestamp queries, if available, cross-check the GPU-side families: a disagreement over 20% with the skip-and-retime figure is reported and explained, not averaged away.

## Method

1. **Bandwidth probe.** Run a WebGPU-only kernel in the same headless Chrome on the same 3060: a large buffer read and reduce. Report the measured GB/s and use it as the roofline, not the spec figure.
2. **Skip-and-retime breakdown,** per stage, decode only: a fixed prompt, greedy decoding, about 128 generated tokens, the median of at least 5 runs. Disable one kernel family at a time (the output may be garbage), re-time the step, and attribute the drop to that family. Pooled's `benchmarks/bench_breakdown.js` is the model for this. The families, adjusted to what the engine actually has:
   - quantized matvecs, split by projection type if possible (attention/DeltaNet in/out, MLP up/gate/down);
   - the DeltaNet recurrent update;
   - attention with the KV cache;
   - norms and activations;
   - the LM head (stage B only);
   - command encoding and submit, measured as CPU time;
   - GPU→CPU readback and hidden-state packing per hop.
3. If the adapter supports timestamp queries, record GPU-side timings as a cross-check.
4. For each matvec family, compute the bytes of weights read per token, and from its time the achieved GB/s as a fraction of the probe's roofline. *(The brief was cut off at "compute the bytes of weights read per"; this ending is assumed and still needs confirming.)*

## Protocol

## Limits

Skip-and-retime assumes the families' costs add up; overlapping GPU work or a skipped kernel changing later kernels' cache behavior can break that, which the timestamp cross-check and the unattributed remainder are meant to expose.
