## Question

Experiment 79 checked our Phi-4 path on a tiny random model with Phi-4's architecture. It left the real checkpoint untested: tensor names, the shard layout, bf16 values, and real activation scales with their outliers. All of these can break the GPU trial (`inference.md` stage 3) and can be checked for free here.

**Do the real checkpoint's layers, read by our reader and run by our kernels, match transformers, and does the whole model give transformers' greedy tokens? How far does bf16 arithmetic, as used on the GPUs, move the result?**

## Prediction (written before the run)

Every layer passes, and the greedy tokens agree. bf16 changes the logits by roughly 0.1–1 and sometimes the chosen token after a few steps. If something fails, the likely cause is the checkpoint's tensor names or its shard index, not the arithmetic.

## Gate (frozen 2026-11-04, before any real-checkpoint run)

The reference is transformers' own modules (`Phi3DecoderLayer`, its rotary embedding, `DynamicCache`, `Phi3RMSNorm`) in float32, with bf16 weights converted exactly. Ours is the NumPy kernels in float64.

1. **Layout:** our reader accepts the checkpoint. Every tensor named in the index has the planned shape, and `config_from_hf` maps the real `config.json`.
2. **Each layer, 40 of 40:**
   - Input: the real hidden states entering that layer, propagated by the reference from the embedding of a fixed 128-token prompt.
   - Prefill check: our output matches transformers' within max |diff| ≤ 1e-4 × max |reference output|.
   - Cached-decode check: an 8-token decode after a 120-token prefill meets the same tolerance.
3. **Whole model:**
   - Reference run: transformers' layers, chained one layer at a time with its cache, generate 16 greedy tokens from each of 3 prompts (2 chat prompts and 1 synthetic).
   - Our run: our kernels in float64 run each full sequence (prompt plus reference tokens) in one pass, one layer at a time.
   - Pass requires the following:
     - our argmax equals the reference's next token at all 48 generated positions;
     - max |logit diff| ≤ 1e-2;
     - one prompt also generated by our greedy loop with our KV cache gives the same 16 tokens.
   - A disagreement is excused only if the reference's top-2 logit margin at that position is below 1e-3, a tie that would be reported as such.
4. **Harness sanity:** on experiment 79's tiny model, the reference's layer-at-a-time chain gives the same logits as transformers' full `Phi3ForCausalLM` (within 1e-5). This shows the chain itself is faithful.
5. **bf16 drift**, measured and not gated: our torch backend in bf16, on the same sequences. Reported for each position: the max |logit diff| from the float32 reference and whether the argmax agrees. Also the first step where free bf16 greedy generation diverges, for each prompt. This sets the tolerances for the GPU trial.

Timing is not gated. The text the 2 chat prompts produce is read as a qualitative check only.

## Protocol

- **Inputs:**
  - `microsoft/phi-4` at revision `2db69c1c3e91a05d2c64a3185acfbaf36f744e25`: 6 safetensors shards, `config.json`, and the tokenizer files.
  - Saved in `runs/models/phi-4/` (local, not in git). Shard hashes are recorded in the evidence.
- **Memory:** at most one layer at a time per side (2.7 GB in float64, 1.4 GB in float32), plus the embedding and the head (4.1 GB in float64) when needed. No accelerate offload: the reference chain is built from transformers' own layer modules.
- **Prompts:**
  - 2 chat prompts through Phi-4's chat template, one short and one about 128 tokens.
  - 1 synthetic prompt of 128 token IDs (seed 2026).
  - The 128-token chat prompt is the layer-check input.
- **Driver:** `python -m ygaraty.inference.phi4_layerwise runs/models/phi-4 --out runs/experiments/WS-10/<host>-<timestamp>/`.
  - Each step loads each layer once and runs it for every session: the reference for the 3 prompts, our float64 greedy loop for prompt 0, and bf16 free greedy for the 3 prompts.
  - One teacher-forced pass follows, for our float64 kernels (uncached) and bf16.
  - The run goes under `watch.py`, with a progress line for each layer.

## Results

**1. Layout: pass.** `Checkpoint` accepts all 243 tensors with their planned shapes (manifest `0f710004e02c`).

**2. Layers: pass, 40 of 40.** Over the 95-token chat prompt's real hidden states, our float64 block against transformers' float32 `Phi3DecoderLayer`, relative to each layer's largest output value:

| Check | Worst layer | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| Prompt pass, uncached | 4.3e-7 | 1e-4 |
| 8-token cached decode after 87 tokens, against transformers' cached decode | 3.4e-7 | 1e-4 |
| The same decode, against transformers' uncached output | 3.4e-7 | (not gated) |

The largest activation was 813. The per-layer values are in the evidence.

**3. Whole model: pass.**

| Prompt | Tokens | Our float64 argmax equals the reference | Max logit diff | Smallest reference top-2 margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chat, short | 19 | 16 of 16 | 4.2e-5 | 0.027 |
| Chat, long | 95 | 16 of 16 | 4.3e-5 | 0.058 |
| Synthetic | 128 | 16 of 16 | 1.2e-5 | 0.170 |

Our greedy loop for the short prompt, with our `LayerCache`, gave the reference's 16 tokens. No position needed the tie exception. The reference's text:
- The short prompt gives "The capital of France is Paris." After that come `<|im_end|><|endoftext|>` and a new user turn, because generation deliberately didn't stop at the end-of-sequence token.
- The long prompt gives "In Ygaraty's distributed neural network system, the upload link typically limits performance".
- The synthetic prompt gives noise, as expected.

**4. Harness check: pass.** On experiment 79's tiny model, transformers' layers chained one at a time give the full `Phi3ForCausalLM`'s greedy tokens, with logits within 1e-5.

**5. bf16 drift (measured, not gated).** Our torch kernels run with bf16 weights and activations on CPU.

| Prompt | Free bf16 greedy against the reference | Teacher-forced argmax agreement | Max logit diff (median over positions) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chat, short | 16 of 16 identical | 16 of 16 | 0.33 (0.17) |
| Chat, long | 16 of 16 identical | 15 of 16 | 0.47 (0.16) |
| Synthetic | 16 of 16 identical | 16 of 16 | 0.10 (0.06) |

The one flip, on the long prompt at position 13, is a tie that bf16 can't represent:
- The reference's top two logits are 23.077 and 23.019.
- bf16 can only step by 0.125 near 23, so both become exactly 23.0, and argmax takes the lower ID.

Free bf16 generation took a different path through the cache arithmetic and kept the reference's token there.

## What it taught

- **The real checkpoint holds no surprises for our path.** Tensor names, the fused projections' split order, the shard index, the bf16 conversion and the real activation scale all agree with transformers. The tiny-model result of experiment 79 carries over to the real weights.
- **bf16 on the GPUs is close enough to keep the reference's tokens.** On these prompts its logits move by 0.1–0.5. Its one mismatch came from bf16 logits themselves: their resolution near the top is 0.125, so close candidates become exact ties. **For the GPU trial, compute the final head's logits in float32** (or at least accumulate them in float32). It costs nothing measurable and removes that kind of flip.
- **A whole 14.7B model runs on a 28 GB CPU machine when loaded one layer at a time.** It takes about 64 s per token for 7 sessions together, limited by reading 29 GB from disk per step. That is useful for correctness, not for serving.
- **The Hub's Xet download stalled here; plain HTTP (`HF_HUB_DISABLE_XET=1`) ran at 11 MB/s.**

## Limits

- **CPU only.** Torch on CUDA and its fused kernels are still untested, as is GPU speed. The bf16 drift here comes from CPU bf16 matrix products, whose accumulation can differ from a GPU's.
- **Three prompts and 16 tokens each.** Tiny margins (the smallest was 0.027) can occur on other prompts, where float32 and float64 could pick different tokens. That would be a tie, not a bug.
- **Not the distributed path.** The whole model never ran on two Ygaraty workers: that needs 29 GB across owners, more than this machine's RAM. The split, the job and the transport were tested on the tiny model in experiment 79.
- **No quantization.** A 4-bit format is still to be chosen and built.
- **The long chat prompt was 95 tokens, not 128.** See the Log.
