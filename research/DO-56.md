## Question

Qwen3.5-9B has 32 layers; only every fourth is full attention, the other 24 are Gated DeltaNet layers whose state has a fixed size. The memory that grows with context is the KV cache of the 8 full-attention layers: 4 KiB per token per layer in fp16, 16 KiB per token per 16-layer stage. At 8k tokens a stage peaked at 3.16 GB of the 4 GB contributor cap (experiment 95), which leaves room for roughly 60k tokens; today a conversation stops at a set capacity and starts over (WS-22).

StreamingLLM (Xiao et al., ICLR 2024, from memory) keeps the first few positions (*attention sinks*) plus a recent window and drops the rest. In Qwen3.5 the DeltaNet layers already carry long-range information, so a capped window on the 8 full-attention layers may cost less than in a pure Transformer. With a cap, a session's memory per stage is fixed and chosen when it starts, and the full-attention layers' compute per token stops growing with context.

**With the full-attention layers' KV capped at 4 sink positions plus a recent window of W positions, how much retrieval and language-modeling quality is lost at 16k–128k tokens of context, and what do memory and time per token look like?**

This leaves exact parity on purpose: beyond the window, tokens differ from the uncapped model. The result is quality against the uncapped model, not identical tokens.

## Prediction

- **Loss on long documents** (mean loss over the last 1,024 tokens of 32k–128k-token documents): within +0.03 nats/token of uncapped at W = 8,192; within +0.01 at 16,384; clearly worse at 2,048.
- **Needle retrieval** (a fact placed at a random depth, asked at the end): near-perfect for needles inside the window; for needles beyond it, above chance only through the DeltaNet layers, 30–70% at W = 8,192. This is the cost.
- **Memory per stage:** fixed at about the weights + 25 MiB of DeltaNet state + (W + 4) × 16 KiB; at W = 8,192 the same as experiment 95's 8k peak, at any context length.
- **Time per token:** flat beyond W, against a slow rise for uncapped (WS-22 saw ~95–120 ms near 16k).

## Gate

TBD, to be frozen before the rented run. Proposed:
- **Mechanics (workstation, S5-nano, required):** with W larger than the sequence, capped and uncapped give identical tokens and caches; with W smaller, the kept positions and their RoPE positions are exactly the planned ones.
- **Counts as usable:** some W ≤ 16,384 loses at most **0.03 nats/token** on long documents at 32k and 64k, against uncapped, with time per token at 64k no more than 5% above that at 8k.
- **Report also:** needle retrieval by depth (inside or beyond the window) and context length; memory per stage; time per token by context length; whether positions are kept absolute or re-indexed within the cache (two arms).

## Protocol

1. **Code:** a `--kv-window W --kv-sinks 4` option for the full-attention layers of the Qwen WebGPU stages: a ring buffer of W positions after the sinks, attention masked to the kept positions. Two position modes: absolute RoPE positions (the model's own), and positions re-indexed within the cache (StreamingLLM's choice beyond training length). Prefill uses the same mask, so its full-attention cost becomes O(n·W) instead of O(n²).
2. **Quality harness (coordinator):** long documents (a fixed set of public-domain books and long code files, about 20 per length), loss over the last 1,024 tokens; needle tasks at 5 depths × 4 lengths × 10 seeds; prompts and documents fixed in the evidence file.
3. **Arms:** uncapped; W ∈ {2,048, 4,096, 8,192, 16,384}; the two position modes at W = 8,192. Context lengths 16k, 32k, 64k, 128k (uncapped up to what fits the 24 GB GPU).
4. **Prepare on the workstation:** the mechanics tests on S5-nano (`tests.test_qwen35_kv_window`), and one long-document loss run on S5-nano to check the harness.
