## Question

Can Kolibri Q4 inference preserve reference behavior while staying within explicitly selected contributor resource commitments at 2/3/4/8 GiB, and what latency and throughput do those distributions deliver?

Contributors must be able to choose an available piece and see what resources and availability it requires before committing. This selection and admission contract is part of the experiment setup, not a separate research backlog item. A piece is a versioned stage in a complete plan, not an arbitrary subset of weights.

Related: [97](097-qwen35-webrtc-direct-links.md), [99](099-user-sessions-webrtc-close-stages.md), [102](102-three-four-worker-plans.md), [103](103-fast-sessions-rollout-rerun.md), and [DO-47](DO-47.md). These establish other model paths, not Kolibri support. [OFF-8](OFF-8.md) studies layer splitting for updates, a different question.

## Prediction

Contiguous complete layers should minimize communication compared with splitting experts across networked workers. Larger memory commitments should reduce sequential boundaries. Smaller contributors may add useful capacity but increase latency; extra workers do not imply faster single-conversation generation.

Kolibri has 50 layers and 78B total parameters, with about 3.46B active per token. Reference sources: [model card](https://huggingface.co/Aleph-Alpha/Kolibri-1) and [config](https://huggingface.co/Aleph-Alpha/Kolibri-1/raw/main/config.json), consulted during planning. Pin exact revisions before execution. The published checkpoint uses FP8; the proposed Q4 conversion and kernels are prerequisites, not existing validated support.

Initial estimates assume one conversation, total cached context at most 8,192 tokens including generation, bounded chunked prefill, packed Q4 storage and tile-wise unpacking. Rough layer storage is 0.8–0.9 GiB including quantization metadata. Reserve roughly 25% for cache, scratch and headroom initially; replace this rule with exact allocation accounting. Endpoint embeddings/head and full-attention KV caches require separate accounting.

| Committed GPU budget | Initial ordinary-stage capacity | Candidate full-pipeline count, including endpoint adjustments |
|---|---|---|
| 2 GiB | 1 layer | 50–52 workers |
| 3 GiB | 2 layers | 25–27 workers |
| 4 GiB | 3 layers | 17–19 workers |
| 8 GiB | 6 layers | 9–10 workers |

These are capacity hypotheses, not certified plans. GPU budget means allocatable memory committed to this workload, not advertised total VRAM. Browser buffer/binding limits and host RAM can invalidate a plan even when aggregate GPU memory appears sufficient.

## Gate

Proposed acceptance structure; numerical tolerances, quality thresholds and performance targets are **TBD and must be frozen before runs**.

1. **Numerical implementation:** validate routing, expert outputs, attention/cache behavior, layers and pipeline logits against a reference using the identical Q4 representation. Fix fixtures and tolerances beforehand. On the frozen greedy suite, require identical tokens across supported partitions and relay/direct routes.
2. **Quantization quality:** separately compare Q4 with the published reference on a fixed evaluation set. Token identity with FP8 is not the quantization-quality criterion; metric and allowed regression are TBD.
3. **Resource commitment:** every admitted piece stays below its declared GPU allocation cap and host-memory bound at the declared maximum context/concurrency and during loading/prefill/decode. Record peak owned GPU buffers separately from process/device-wide measurements. Insufficient device limits or budgets reject the piece before loading; do not silently borrow memory or change assignments.
4. **Contributor selection:** only compatible available pieces are selectable; concurrent attempts to claim the same slot resolve atomically. Show an explicit acceptance summary before loading. A contributor never receives a different or larger piece without selecting it. Expired reservations reopen; a pipeline serves only after all required stages are ready.
5. **Lifecycle:** cancel, leave and replacement release resources and preserve session correctness or return an explicit bounded failure. No silent hang or commitment extension. Freeze reservation, connection, drain and recovery timeouts before execution.
6. **Performance:** report time to first token, median/p95 decode latency, aggregate tokens/s, bytes transferred and cost per generated token for each admitted pool. No speedup claim until matched measurements exist; target latency and cost ceilings are TBD.

## Protocol

### Contributor piece selection and commitment

The contributor first selects a resource budget. Present compatible open pieces with the following information, distinguishing estimates from measured requirements:

- Model/checkpoint and quantization version, plan/pipeline ID, piece ID, layer range, endpoint responsibilities, and available/reserved/loading/ready status.
- Required peak GPU allocation; host RAM for download, staging and decoding; persistent cache/disk space; initial download size and estimated cold-start time. Account for transient duplicate buffers, not just final weights.
- Supported context and session count; estimated compute duty cycle, sustained upload/download needs and traffic allowance; measured link latency where available. Unknown values remain explicitly unknown until calibration.
- Requested availability window, reservation expiry, and expected drain time when leaving. The contributor may leave early; show the service impact. Duration and traffic limits must not silently renew.
- Route choice and its implications: direct WebRTC versus relay, including peer-address exposure for direct connections. TURN-only is a separate route and must not be assumed validated by experiment 103.

Flow: inspect available pieces → select piece and resource limits → review commitment → reserve slot → check capabilities and required links → load within caps → ready. Release the reservation on admission/loading failure. Resource enforcement must back the displayed commitment: allocation checks, bounded prefill and session admission, transfer accounting, stop/drain controls and cleanup. GPU memory limits do not imply hard GPU-utilization or power caps; unsupported controls must not be advertised as guarantees.

All pieces in a pipeline must share the same immutable model/plan contract. Keep a layer's experts and KV state on its owner. Streaming missing expert weights over the network per token and CPU compute workers are outside this experiment. Expert splitting can be considered later, but is not a prerequisite for these plans.

### Ordered execution stages

1. **Prepare and validate on an authorized execution host.** Implement Kolibri loading, Q4 conversion/kernels, MoE routing/combination, sliding/global attention, tokenizer/output handling and stage manifests. Produce a native reference only as a numerical oracle; all experimental contributor compute is WebGPU. Pin checkpoint, conversion, browser, kernel and fixture versions. Do not download inputs or execute workloads on the offline simulation machine.
2. **First full-model pool: 10 × 8 GiB.** Start with five consecutive layers per stage. Each five-layer group contains one full-attention layer. Check embedding/head overhead and loading peaks before admitting this split; if it does not fit, record that result and revise explicitly rather than exceeding commitments. Use distinct physical hosts for the distributed measurement; multiple tabs on one GPU are only a preliminary correctness check.
3. **Mixed 4/8 GiB pools.** Freeze exact splits after memory accounting. One capacity candidate is seven 8 GiB workers owning six layers each and three 4 GiB workers owning 3, 3 and 2 layers, totaling 50. Place endpoints and full-attention layers only where their actual budgets fit. Compare against the first pool, reporting both hardware changes and stage-count effects.
4. **2/3 GiB admission.** Validate individual one-/two-layer pieces, including full-attention and endpoint cases where feasible, then replace selected larger stages with compatible smaller stages in a complete pipeline. A successful piece check does not establish an all-2-GiB or all-3-GiB pipeline. Those larger fleets are conditional extensions, not part of the initial rental commitment.
5. **Transport matrix.** Start with measured close links (target 1–3 ms RTT), comparing direct WebRTC and relay on the same hosts with interleaved repeated runs. Record route candidates, RTT, bandwidth, jitter, load and GPU clocks. Later 10–20 ms and 40–60 ms pairs are conditional on budget. Country labels alone are not latency evidence.
6. **Workload and lifecycle matrix.** Freeze short and long prompts, output lengths, repeat count and seeds before timing. Start at concurrency one, then bounded concurrency with new cache accounting. Exercise competing claims, expired reservations, inadequate resources, cancellation, early departure, replacement and cleanup. Distinguish cold loading, connection setup, prefill and steady decode.

### Implementation (2026-11-10, offline)

What exists, and what it has and has not been checked against:

- **Weights.** `ygaraty/inference/kolibri_convert.py` turns the official FP8 checkpoint into our own GGUF (upstream llama.cpp has no `kolibri1`): routed experts Q4_K, attention, the shared expert, the embedding and the head Q6_K, norms, the router and its selection bias float32. FP8 blocks are dequantized as transformers does (`weight * weight_scale_inv`); `kquant.py` is a NumPy port of llama.cpp's reference Q4_K/Q6_K search (no importance matrix). Tensor names come from the checkpoint's index and the first shard's header (per-expert `gate_proj`/`up_proj`/`down_proj` with float32 `weight_scale_inv` [rows/128, cols/128]; `post_attn_norm`, `post_ffn_norm`, `moe.router.expert_bias`, mapped as transformers 5.22's `conversion_mapping` maps them).
- **Family.** `kolibri1` in `families.py`; loader and NumPy reference `kolibri_gguf.py`; browser stage `kolibri_gpu.mts` with five new plan operations (`moe_route`, `moe_matmul`, `silu_mul_halves`, `moe_combine`, `add`) and `windowed_attention`; the compiler's `KolibriComposition`. The expert product reads its slot's expert id on the GPU and decodes only that expert's rows; each layer's experts are chunks of at most 128 MiB.
- **Checked offline.** `tests/test_kolibri_family.py`: a tiny random `Kolibri1ForCausalLM` (3 layers, a 3-key window, 8 experts, top 2, random selection bias) written in the checkpoint's own layout, FP8 included, converted, then the NumPy reference against transformers' full model: logits within 1e-5 relative over 9 tokens; chunked prefill equals one pass; the converter's tensor mapping. `tests/webgpu/kolibri.test.mjs`: the compiled plan issues the stage's direct-call reference layer exactly, for prefill, single tokens and batches. Bundles for a tiny model prepare, and the coordinator loads them.
- **Not checked.** No WGSL has been compiled by a browser or by naga yet; no real checkpoint converted; no GPU run; no quantization-quality measurement (gate 2 needs its own tool and an FP8 reference run). Prefill computes each (token, expert) slot as a matrix-vector product, correct but slower than a grouped matrix product. Direct (WebRTC) plans are limited to 2-4 workers by the user panel, so Kolibri plans use the relay.

### Handoff: commands on the execution host

Proposed numerical gate (freeze, or change, before the first run): WS-12's. `kernels` within 1e-5 of the largest output; `stage` per-row error at most 1e-3 of the row's largest reference value and cosine at least 0.99999, except rows where the reference's routing margin (`routing_margin_min` in the report) is below 1e-3 (a near-tie can pick another expert); `pipeline` every greedy token equal to the reference's except near ties (margin below 0.05), logits within 2e-3.

```sh
pip install -e '.[verification]'   # torch and transformers >= 5.22 for the offline tests and cross-checks
python -m pytest tests/test_kolibri_family.py && npm ci && npm run test:webgpu
M=runs/models/kolibri-1; O=runs/experiments/DO-48/$(hostname)-$(date -u +%Y%m%dT%H%M)
python -m ygaraty.inference.kolibri_convert pin --out $M/source.json
ygaraty model fetch $M/source.json --dir $M                                  # about 80 GB
python -m ygaraty.inference.kolibri_convert convert --dir $M --out $M/kolibri-1-Q4_K.gguf \
  --source $M/source.json --workers $(nproc)                                   # about 45 GB
python -m ygaraty.inference.kolibri_convert record --dir $M --gguf kolibri-1-Q4_K.gguf \
  --source $M/source.json --out $M/model.json
G="--gguf $M/kolibri-1-Q4_K.gguf --config $M/config.json"
python -m ygaraty.experiments.qwen35_webgpu kernels $G --out $O/kernels
python -m ygaraty.experiments.qwen35_webgpu stage $G --tokenizer $M/tokenizer.json --first 0 --last 5 \
  --chunks 9,1,1,1 --kv f32 --out $O/stage-0-5
python -m ygaraty.experiments.qwen35_webgpu stage $G --tokenizer $M/tokenizer.json --first 45 --last 50 \
  --chunks 9,1,1,1 --kv f32 --out $O/stage-45-50
python -m ygaraty.experiments.qwen35_webgpu pipeline $G --tokenizer $M/tokenizer.json \
  --splits 0,5,10,15,20,25,30,35,40,45,50 --generate 32 --kv f16 --wire f16 --out $O/pipeline
ygaraty model plan $M/model.json --workers 8,9,10 --capacity 4096
ygaraty model prepare $M/model.json --dir $M --workers 10 --root MODEL_ROOT --bundle-root kolibri-1/v1
ygaraty model fixture $M/model.json --id kolibri-1 --name Kolibri-1 --bundle-root kolibri-1/v1 \
  --workers 10 --pk UNUSED_PK > $O/kolibri-1-fixture.json
```

`pipeline` loads every stage into one browser (about 42 GB of weights): it needs a GPU with at least 48 GB (an 80 GB H100 or A100). On smaller GPUs run `stage` for each five-layer group in turn (the reference provides each group's input) and the `contributors` mode, which has no reference. The first `stage` run is also the first time the shaders compile: a WGSL error there is an implementation fault to fix before anything else. Then serve the 10-worker bundle with `scripts/user_session_check.py` (relay route) on distinct hosts for step 2.

## Limits

### Preparation results (2026-11-11)

All 50 layers converted in 8,124.5 seconds. Layer weights total 41.19 GiB plus
0.50 GiB for embedding/head. At context capacity 4096, the ten-worker planner
estimates 4.17 GiB per middle stage and 4.42 GiB at the endpoints, recommending
6 GiB per worker. These are estimates, not measured browser peaks.

Preparation used 8 CPUs, 31 GiB RAM, 8 conversion processes and one BLAS/OpenMP
thread per process. The pinned model revision was
`abfe85f673fd79a3e1cdb98289e7a1c635345ee1` (32 shards, 73.4 GiB). Conversion used
Q4_K experts, Q6_K dense tensors and a 20-step search. Ten-worker splits were
`0,5,10,15,20,25,30,35,40,45,50`, with capacity 4096 and a 256-token generation limit.

Bundles for 1/2/3/4/10 workers were subsequently exported and published, with
manifest hashes checked and metadata validated using `PreparedSession`.
Configured budgets were 49/25/17/13/6 GiB per worker respectively. Publication
and metadata validation do not establish GPU fit or numerical parity; the
single-contributor GPU measurement is recorded in experiment 113 above.

A separate one-CPU, roughly 2 GiB machine with 84 GiB free disk was unsuitable
for conversion: source plus output exceeded disk capacity and converter
allocations exceeded its practical memory budget. Private deployment and
publication operations are archived outside the public repository.

Memory arithmetic does not prove allocation feasibility, numerical quality or token latency. Artificial budget caps on large GPUs do not establish performance on actual small-memory devices. Results on nearby rented GPUs do not establish volunteer-network behavior. This experiment makes no training claim and does not certify arbitrary long-context or concurrent use.
