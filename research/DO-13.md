## Question

In DiLoCo-style training, every learner stops at the outer step until the averaged pseudo-gradient arrives. Kale et al. (2025, arXiv:2502.12996) overlap that exchange with the next inner steps. Each learner applies its own local pseudo-gradient as a stand-in at once, and corrects with the averaged one when it lands, which takes the outer communication off the critical path.

In DO-12's hybrid islands, the outer step waits for both islands' owners to publish, for the average, and for the reload. A slow island also stalls the other.

**With eager outer updates, do two islands reach DO-12's target loss sooner in wall time than with the blocking outer step, at the same H?**

Needs DO-12 first: it builds the islands and measures the blocking outer step's cost.

## Prediction

- **Blocking cost:** DO-12's outer step costs a few seconds per outer round: owners publish 2–8 MB each over 20–100 Mb/s, plus the barrier.
- **At H = 25:** that is a few percent of wall time, so eager updates gain under 5%.
- **At smaller H** (more frequent averaging, which DO-12 may show learns better), the gain grows.
- **Learning:** the loss at equal pairs is within 0.02 of the blocking version, as the paper reports for language models.

## Protocol

- **Implementation:** DO-12's outer process, with `YGARATY_OUTER_EAGER=1`.
  - Islands continue inner updates after publishing.
  - The averaged correction is applied to the owners' weights at the next inner update boundary after it arrives, as the paper's eager variant does.
- **Arms:** blocking against eager, at DO-12's two H values; one run each, learning curves from published snapshots. About 3 hours of experiment 61's pool, about $8–10.
- **Prepare on the workstation first:** a loopback test with 2 replicas of `ygaraty.diloco` showing that eager and blocking give the same weights when the averaged pseudo-gradient equals the local one.
