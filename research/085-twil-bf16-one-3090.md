## Question

[Experiment 83](083-twil-int4-four-capped-gpus.md) served 4-bit TwIL-8B from four capped GPUs at 0.95 s per token. **On one "real" GPU in bf16, does the pipeline give the bf16 reference's tokens, and how fast is it?** That is the quality and speed the 4 GB setup gives up.

## Prediction

- **Tokens:** equal to the workstation's bf16 reference (experiment 83's `reference.json`), up to the first position with a top-2 margin below 0.5. GPU and CPU bf16 kernels round differently, so a near-tie may diverge.
- **Speed:** 17.6 GB of weights read per token at about 936 GB/s is at least 19 ms per token; at experiment 81's efficiency, about 25–30 ms, with no network.
- **Memory:** 17.6 GB of weights plus 164 KB of cache per token, so 16k tokens of context fit in 24 GB.

## Gate (frozen 2026-11-05, before any machine was made; the user's yes)

1. **Correctness:**
   - Experiment 83's 3 chat prompts, 16 greedy tokens each.
   - Pass if each prompt's tokens equal the bf16 reference up to its first position with a top-2 margin below 0.5. A divergence at or after such a position counts as a tie and is reported.
   - Every job completes, with 0 rejected results.
2. **Measurement** (reported):
   - Prompts of 128, 1,024 and 8,192 tokens (seed 2026), 128 new tokens, 1 warmup plus 3 measured.
   - Reported: median time to first token, median and p95 inter-token latency, peak GPU memory.
   - Compared with experiment 83's 0.95 s per token.

## What it taught

- **The pipeline is exact on the GPU.** Its bf16 tokens equal transformers' bf16 tokens on the same RTX 3090, all 48. The departure from the reference is the reference's device: bf16 on a CPU and on a GPU accumulate differently, enough to move a margin from 1.5 to 0.375.
  - A CPU reference only bounds GPU bf16 runs at much larger margins than 0.5.
  - A gate for GPU bf16 should compare against transformers on the same GPU, or against a float32 reference.
- **One GPU in bf16 is the speed baseline: 50 ms per token**, against the predicted 25–30 ms.
  - That is about 350 GB/s of weight reads, 38% of the card's bandwidth.
  - The rest is the step path's per-token coordination on one machine, and Python. Experiment 84's persistent sessions remove the coordination part.
- **The 4 GB setup gives up about 19× in speed** (0.95 s against 50 ms per token) and changes some first tokens (experiment 83). Most of the loss is the network across four hosts, not the 4-bit weights.
- **Setup of a full bf16 checkpoint takes 12 min** (17.6 GB in 655 s), against 5 min for four range-downloading machines in experiment 83.

## Limits

- One GPU type, one host, and one job per prompt length (3 measured sessions each).
- The gate's reference was CPU bf16; no float32 reference exists (8.8B in float32 needs 35 GB of RAM, more than the workstation has).
- Peak memory is from nvidia-smi samples, CUDA context included.
- Cost is estimated from the hourly price and Vast.ai's per-GB rate, not read from the invoice.
